Tail Risk Spillovers & Portfolio Resilience

Investigated tail-risk spillovers and portfolio resilience in the South African equity market using a Quantile Factor Vector Autoregression (QFVAR) framework. Utilizing packages such as statsmodels, networkx, and arch, the study quantified systemic risk transmission and developed network-augmented strategies to enhance resilience during extreme market conditions.

Key findings
  • Systemic connectedness intensifies substantially during both bearish and bullish market extremes, forming an asymmetric U-shaped pattern.
  • Financial institutions and consumer goods serve as net transmitters of shocks, while technology and mining equities typically absorb shocks as net receivers.
  • Network-augmented portfolio strategies significantly outperformed traditional mean-variance and CVaR models in risk-adjusted returns and crisis resilience.

Research, macroeconomics

Inflation Dynamics & Economic Growth

Examined the impact of the SARB's inflation-targeting framework on South Africa's economic growth using an Autoregressive Distributed Lag (ARDL) model and an Error Correction Model (ECM) to establish short- and long-run associations.

Key findings
  • Established a positive short-run and definitive long-run relationship between economic growth, final consumption expenditure, and imports.
  • Found that the SARB's inflation-targeting framework successfully maintained price stability but ultimately slowed down overall economic growth.
  • Recommended broadening the targeted inflation rate to better balance price stability with sustainable economic expansion.
